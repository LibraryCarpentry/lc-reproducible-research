## Outcome-to-assessment map

| Outcome | Where assessed |
|---|---|
| Distinguish reproduction, replication, generalization | Understanding Reproducibility's two classification challenges (unchanged) |
| Distinguish reproduction/replication from openness, with evidence | Making Research Checkable's "Reproduced, replicated, or just open?" challenge (kept, unchanged) |
| Given a reported result, identify what connects it to its materials/decisions, and name what's missing | Making Research Checkable's new "What connects to what?" evidence-mapping challenge (replaces "Now you construct the examples") |
| Given a workflow gap, identify a practice/artifact, propose an improvement, and describe what check it enables | Tools' extended "What would you ask for - and what would you do next?" (replaces the shorter version) and the new "Before you go" transfer task (replaces "Name four") |
| Describe a specific task a documentation tool and a version-control tool would each be used for | Tools' "What would go wrong here?" (codebook) and the Git version-control scenario (both unchanged) |
| Given a researcher's request, recommend a proportionate check/action and identify who could carry it out (including a librarian directly) | Library Role's "Which service applies?" three-line note and the new direct-technical-contribution language in "How Libraries Support Reproducibility" |

Landing-page and Introduction objectives were reworded to match this map (see `index.md` and `episodes/introduction.md` diffs) without adding a fourth outcome.

## What changed, and what replaced what

- **Making Research Checkable**: introduced a small fictional packet (`survey.csv` + the existing codebook entry, `analysis.R` said to produce a named figure, and an interview claim tied to an approved excerpt and an analytic note). Replaced "Now you construct the examples" (~4 min, repeated classification work, and contained an overclaim - "no one...could redo the analysis" - flagged by the plan) with "What connects to what?" (~5 min), which asks what's missing to reproduce the figure and to trace the interview claim. Kept "Reproduced, replicated, or just open?" as the episode's one openness/reproducibility assessment. Objectives and keypoints updated to match; front-matter exercise time now matches required activities exactly (10 = 5 + 5, no more unlabeled buffer).
- **Tools**: reuses the packet (explicit callback added). Extended "What would you ask for?" (~4→~6 min) to also require a proposed improvement and a statement of what check it enables - learners may request missing facts or write explicit placeholders, never invent a version or requirement. Removed "Which tool would you reach for?" (~2 min) as redundant with "What would go wrong here?", freeing time for the extension. Replaced "Name four" (~2 min, tool-recall) with "Before you go" (~3 min): a gap, a practice/artifact, and what it does or doesn't establish. The tools-list objective was reworded to match (no longer requires naming four tools by name); tool names still appear throughout as examples. Loosened the "quantitative = rerun, qualitative = trace" framing in "Can Someone Trace the Reported Findings?" - both need traceable evidence, a rerun is one check type usable in either kind of project, and a project can need more than one.
- **Library Role**: removed the categorical "not the librarian" exclusion and the "usually a stretch...rather than a solo library service" framing (finding 1 in the plan). Replaced with task-specific scope (teach / implement / collaborate / assess, by skill, access, and capacity) and one added concrete direct-technical bullet (comparing committed script versions; restoring a documented environment from a lockfile). The reflection's "possible answers" and the diagram's return-to-process caption were reworded the same way. Fixed the "documentation is missing" overclaim in the first scenario - Dr. Torres's message now explicitly says she has scripts and notes but nothing organized, so the solution can honestly say "look at what she has first" instead of assuming a gap that wasn't established.
- **Introduction / index.md**: reworded the practical-outcome sentence to match the prompt's exact learning outcome (identify what's needed, recommend an improvement, explain who could carry out/assess the check) and to explicitly distinguish *checkable* from *checked*. Softened the "depositing three artifacts makes reproduction possible" line - a missing dependency or unclear step can still block it; deposit makes reproduction *possible*, a successful rerun *confirms* it. Added one clause acknowledging open science's wider concerns (participation, equitable access) beyond output availability, without expanding into a survey. `index.md`'s three learning objectives reworded to match the map above.
- **The Mermaid diagram** (Introduction + Library Role, the only two occurrences): redesigned per the plan's finding 4 and the prompt's step 7. New flow: result/output → identify materials/decisions → is the connection traceable? → (gap: recommend a specific improvement with an owner, loop back) / (traceable: plan a proportionate check - rerun, version comparison, or evidence-tracing, explicitly not sorted by quantitative/qualitative) → someone performs the check → agree the next action. This separates *planning* a check from *performing* one, and drops the old quantitative-rerun/qualitative-trace branching that implied a rigid divide. Library Role's occurrence was previously missing its accessible text-equivalent callout (the plan caught this); both occurrences now have matching ordered-list callouts.
